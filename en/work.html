01 Concept project Bike repair workshop Starter level

Kaari Bike Works

The Starter package example: one long page for a fictional bike workshop in Kallio, Helsinki. Everything important is on the same page, and the top menu jumps straight to services, prices, how it works, location and the booking form.

Design direction

Swiss and direct. Narrow cuts of Archivo for headlines, a paper-white surface, black text and a cobalt-blue accent. No ornament, just a clear order.

Key features

  • One page, six sections: services, prices, how it works, about, visit, booking
  • Anchor menu that highlights the section you are in
  • Price list with example prices
  • Contact details, opening hours and a booking form

Design decisions

  • Everything on one page, as in the Starter package
  • Two images are enough when the typography carries it
  • Same order on a phone, with an easy menu
  • Clearly labelled as fictional, prices are examples

02 Concept project Physiotherapy Business level

Ankkuri Physiotherapy

The Business package example: a five-page site for a fictional physiotherapy clinic in Turku. Home, services with a price list, about, gallery and contact with a booking request form, each on its own page.

Design direction

Calm and warm. Young Serif for headlines, Figtree for text, a sand-coloured surface, navy and coral. Soft corners and plenty of space so the site feels trustworthy.

Key features

  • Five pages: home, services, about, gallery, contact
  • Each service explained, plus a clear price list
  • Image gallery of the clinic
  • Booking request form, opening hours and FAQ

Design decisions

  • Every page has its own title and description for search engines
  • Clear navigation that shows the current page
  • Gentle reveals, no heavy effects
  • No health claims and no invented reviews

03 Concept project Barber Business level

Hale Barber Studio

A five-page concept for an independent barbershop. The site is built around a quiet hour in the chair: bone, forest green, and photographs of the room, not stock smiles.

Design direction

Editorial and calm, and no longer in the studio’s own display face. Cormorant Garamond for the voice, Outfit for body text, a bone surface, forest green and a brass monogram.

Main features

  • Five pages: home, services, barbers, gallery, visit
  • A service menu with example prices
  • A staff introduction and a close-in gallery of the room
  • A visit page that does not pretend to take real appointments

Design decisions

  • A typographic wordmark instead of a drawn logo
  • Photographs of the room and the work, not lifestyle poses
  • A booking path that stays honest as a concept

04 Concept project Automotive detailing Business level

Alto Detail

A dark showroom concept for a detailing studio: graphite, oxide red, and a cinematic opening with the car and the headline in the same frame. Five pages, including a results page whose sliders are the main object.

Key feature. The same car, twice. The sliders compare two treatments of the same photograph. They are illustrative, not evidence of paid work.

Design direction

Cinematic and industrial. Big Shoulders for an automotive voice, Source Sans 3 for reading, a graphite ground and an oxide-red accent — closer to a night bay than to a light-grey app.

Main features

  • Five pages: home, services, results, process, contact
  • Three named packages with example prices
  • Working before-and-after sliders, described honestly
  • A process page that walks through a day in the bay

Design decisions

  • A typographic mark that reads like a manufacturer’s name
  • Comparisons that admit the “before” frame is processed
  • Prices on the page, not behind a quote form

05 Concept project Café Business level

Maison Café

A five-page café concept on parchment, bottle green and copper. The site is built like a short publication: a table, a menu with food photography, a room study, a story, and a visit page that admits it lives only in the story.

Menu

The room

Warm and typographic. Bodoni Moda for the display voice, Manrope for body text, a parchment surface, bottle-green bands and a copper accent.

Main features

  • Five pages: home, menu, the room, story, visit
  • An editorial menu with example prices

  • A strong serif as the café’s voice

06 Concept project Holiday cabins in Lapland Premium level

Kaamos Cabins

The Premium package example: a seven-page site for fictional design cabins near Inari. It is built to feel like the trip itself: the aurora moves on the home page, headlines rise into view and pages hand over to each other softly.

Design direction

Dark and cinematic, like the polar night. Syne for headlines, Hanken Grotesk for text, a night-blue surface, aurora green and the amber of window light. Large images and bold layouts.

Key features

  • Seven pages: home, cabins, experiences, seasons, gallery, story, request dates
  • Tabbed cabin selector and experiences filtered by season
  • Winter–summer switch and a month-by-month daylight guide
  • Gallery with a lightbox and a request form that shows an estimate

Design decisions

  • Smooth page transitions, parallax and rising headlines
  • Animation kept light so the site stays fast
  • Less motion for visitors who prefer reduced motion
  • A request form, not a booking system, and labelled as fictional

07 Concept project Olive oils and fine foods Signature level

Maison Olivier

A one-page premium concept for a fictional olive oil and fine-food boutique in Grasse. The page is built to feel like an experience: products turn on a wheel as you scroll, each product has its own colour, and the oil pour plays in step with the scroll. The brand, products and prices are fictional.

Design direction

Editorial and sensory. Fraunces serif for headlines, Inter for the interface, and a colour for each product: olive green, saffron, terracotta, sage, cream and lavender.

Main features

  • A product wheel that turns with scroll, arrows or swipe
  • Background colour and large title change with each product
  • A scroll-driven video of olive oil pouring
  • AI-generated product photos, mood images and video

Design decisions

  • Motion presents the product instead of competing with it
  • Works on mobile with a swipe
  • Motion is reduced when the visitor prefers less motion
  • Clearly labelled as a fictional brand, prices are examples
